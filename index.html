How to play

The goal

Build an airline from a few small planes into a network. There is no final win screen. You keep growing through the chapters, and the only way to lose is bankruptcy: cash below zero for 4 weeks in a row. Trip Calloway of Gadfly Air is rooting for that.

Every week

1. Buy a plane (Market tab, used or new). 2. Open a route (Routes tab, Open a route). 3. Schedule the plane on it: use the + and - buttons on the route card or the plane card. 4. Set fares. 5. Tap Next week.

Pricing

Each route has a business fare and a leisure fare. Business travelers care less about price but fill seats first. Leisure travelers are price shy. Research on a route shows its real demand, so you can find the fares that earn the most. Tap a fare to type it in.

Your network

Hub-and-spoke: every route touches a hub, licenses are cheaper, and the more routes a hub has the bigger its feed bonus, but each hub gets crowded. From Chapter 2 you can buy a second hub (Routes, Open a route) to spread the crowding and open more routes. Watch for overlap: two routes into the same city share its demand, and hubs less than 500 miles apart poach each other. Point-to-point: any route that touches a city you already serve, full-price licenses, no crowding.

The world

The world gets bigger as you advance. Fast aircraft may only keep their top speed over water, so check the specs when you shop.

Cabins and reputation

In the Hangar you can fit each plane with Wi-Fi, screens, a premium cabin or extra legroom. Each one lifts demand on routes long enough to notice it, and some cost seats. Put your upgraded planes where they matter. More round trips a week also draw more travelers, especially business ones. Your reputation (0 to 100) moves slowly with how you run things and lifts or cuts demand on every route.

Keeping planes flying

Planes wear out with flying hours. Repair them in the Hangar (each plane has a limited number of repairs). Fleet programs improve repairs and fuel use for the whole fleet, for a one-time price plus a weekly fee. The fuel and maintenance cards show what they save and how long they take to pay back. Factory-new planes get cheaper as your airline grows. Check the Market for occasional clearance sales.

Fares and the Revenue Manager

From Chapter 2 you can bring in Dot for 4, 8 or 12 weeks. She reprices your researched routes every week for a fee per route, then leaves and your old fares come back. A senior consultant arrives later and gets closer to the best fares for more money. Careful hand pricing still beats both. The All fares +/-5% buttons on the Routes tab move every fare at once.

Money

Fuel, crew and airport fees come out of every route. The Money tab shows exactly where cash went, and lets you borrow from three lenders. Debt counts against your net worth and your payments come out every week.

Events

Storms, conventions, festivals, fuel swings and recessions come and go. The banner under your stats shows what is happening and what is coming. Some warn you first and some do not.

Goals

Chapters unlock new cities and bigger planes when you meet their goals. Achievements pay cash bonuses: open the Goals tab and tap Claim.

Notes and saving

The game saves in this browser automatically. The Notes button lets you write down anything that felt off, and copy it all out in one go.
